What to look at first

Start with shape, spacing, and curb appeal.

Before choosing a brand or glass package, look at how each opening sits on the house. Taller windows can make a room feel brighter, paired windows can balance a wide wall, and dark or light trim can completely change the exterior. These photos are useful for pointing out the look you want before measurements begin.

Double hung windows

The classic choice for many rooms.

Double hung windows have two moving sashes, so they work well where homeowners want familiar style, flexible airflow, and easier cleaning options. They are common in bedrooms, living rooms, and traditional home exteriors.

Compare double hung windows

Picture windows

More glass when the view matters.

Picture windows do not open, but they can bring in more light and create a cleaner view. They are often paired with operating windows nearby so the room still gets ventilation.

Explore home window options

Entry and patio doors

Doors should fit the way the home is used.

A front door can change the first impression of the house, while a patio door affects daily movement to the backyard, deck, or porch. As you browse, notice glass size, sidelights, grids, hardware color, and how much privacy each style gives from the street.

See door options

Installation details

Good results are in the edges.

Clean caulk lines, even trim, square corners, and smooth operation are the details that make replacement windows feel finished. Those details also help protect the opening from drafts and water over time.

Choosing a mix

Not every room needs the same window.

A home might use double hung windows in bedrooms, a picture window in the living room, sliders where reach is limited, and a patio door near the kitchen. The best plan usually matches the product to the room.
